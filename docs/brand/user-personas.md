# Arpli: user personas, brand and voice view

Six personas, each a way of applying. This file is the **brand and voice view**: how each one hears Arpli. The evidence (rows, sources, behaviour, needs, conditions) lives in [`design/13-personas.md`](../../design/13-personas.md). Where the two differ, `design/13-personas.md` wins.

These are **working personas**, built from public research only (forums, surveys, programme pages, studies), not yet from conversations with real applicants. They expire on **15 May 2027**, or sooner, once real applicants have been heard in Test.

Guiding principle: Arpli users apply from a position of **hope mixed with vulnerability**. These personas keep that vulnerability in view so it never gets designed away.

---

## How to use these personas

- **Before designing a screen,** pick a persona and walk through it as them. Then walk it again with one of the conditions switched on (second language, little on paper, applying from Nigeria or Africa, applying again, changing direction, no one to ask).
- **When writing copy,** ask: does this persona hear "Arpli is for me"?
- **When reviewing,** name the persona a decision serves, and the one it doesn't.
- **The quotes are real.** Each one comes from a real person who applies that way, with its source in `design/13-personas.md`. The personas themselves never "say" anything.
- **No primary persona yet.** The owner picks one in Prioritize, after outcomes have been scored for each persona.

## Ada · the funded-study campaigner

**How she applies:** one year-long campaign for fully funded study abroad, built around one or two flagship scholarships, with sets of essays that must read as one story, referees and offers to line up, and about 1% odds. A graduate with some years of work, applying from Lagos.

**In the words of someone who applies like her:**
> "My biggest fear is spending an entire year preparing applications and still ending up without an admission or scholarship."

**She wants to feel** that her ordinary record is enough, and that the year wasn't wasted, even after a no. **She wants to be seen as** a genuine person in her own voice, not a perfect candidate and not like every other applicant.

**How Ada hears Arpli**
- **Affirmation without flattery.** "That's a specific example of leadership" lands; "amazing!!" doesn't. Reviewers reward the specific and checkable, and so does she.
- **The empty state is the hardest moment.** Never leave her staring at a blank box. Offer a specific way in, built from what she has already told Arpli.
- **Her English is not an error.** Idioms from US or UK culture may not be hers, and Commonwealth English is correct English. Tidy without changing her voice.
- **Steady on a bad connection.** A dropped request never loses her draft, and nothing alarming appears when it happens.

**What "Arpli worked" would look like** (a target, not a finding): she starts this year's campaign from last year's, not from a blank page. The essays tell one story. Every line is one she could say at interview. A no still leaves her better set up for the next one.

## Sam · the volume job seeker

**How he applies:** many small bets every week for months: CV re-keyed into forms, the same screening questions, cover letters, with silence pushing the count up. Often out of work, or afraid of being.

**In the words of someone who applies like him:**
> "I only do Workday apps if it's something I REALLY want."

**He wants to feel** in control of a numbers game he doesn't control, and steady while money runs down. **He wants to be seen as** a real, thoughtful person, not a bot, and to sound the same on the phone as on the page.

**How Sam hears Arpli**
- **Fast, never careless.** He has no time, but one wrong fact sent in his name ends his trust. Speed comes from not retyping, not from skipping his look.
- **Steady about the numbers.** Never cheerful about counts of rejections or silence. One steady line, then the next thing to do.
- **No billing tricks.** No surprise renewals, no upsell in the middle of a task. He is often paying out of savings.
- **Plain about what was sent.** He wants to know what went where, with which CV, in words, not decoration.

**What "Arpli worked" would look like** (a target, not a finding): a question he has answered before takes seconds, in words that fit this employer. He always knows what he sent where. He never has to choose between care and volume.

## Maya · the small-awards grinder

**How she applies:** 25 to 30 small scholarships through the year, the same prompts and word counts over and over, fitted around a part-time job and classes. US college student.

**In the words of someone who applies like her:**
> "Same prompts, same word counts, same everything. My fingers are cramping from typing variations of my part-time job story."

**She wants to feel** that the hours pay off, and that her ordinary story is enough. **She wants to be seen as** genuine and specific, not one more generic essay.

**How Maya hears Arpli**
- **Small first steps.** Reflective prompts make people like her feel "out of my depth". Offer one small question first, never a big blank box.
- **Her part-time job is evidence.** Name what it shows, plainly, without inflating it. Affirmation without flattery, as with Ada.
- **Her time is a shift at work.** Every screen has to earn its place, because the alternative is more paid hours.
- **The core is free.** Never a paywall between her and her next application.

**What "Arpli worked" would look like** (a target, not a finding): one story, told across 30 prompts in her own words. She gets started in minutes, not hours, and always sees what's due and what's missing.

## Emeka · the programme-chasing founder

**How he applies:** a handful of accelerators, competitions and programmes each cycle, in fixed windows, often re-applying to the same flagship; short answers, videos, a deck and numbers, often with co-founders, while running the company. Applies from Abuja.

**In the words of someone who applies like him:**
> "Something like 12 hours even though this is my second application with the same idea."

**He wants to feel** that a no isn't a verdict on him or the idea. **He wants to be seen as** ambitious but honest: a team that sounds like itself.

**How Emeka hears Arpli**
- **Speed over ceremony.** Keyboard first, tab to advance, undo instead of "are you sure?".
- **Dense when he asks for it.** He is an experienced applicant and can take dense views.
- **No condescension.** He has applied many times and read every tips post. Don't repeat them.
- **Plain, not flowery.** Founders clean up "flowery" AI output and keep answers that sound "more like 'us'". Arpli's suggestions read like a founder wrote them.

**What "Arpli worked" would look like** (a target, not a finding): one company story across formats and cycles, showing what's changed since last time. Hours back for building. A no leaves the material ready for the next programme and the investor deck.

## Ngozi · the fellowship chaser on a contract

**How she applies:** a few large, high-stakes bids a year, timed by funder calls and a fixed-term contract that's ending: fellowships and first grants, long sections, a narrative CV, internal deadlines weeks early, rejected work reshaped and sent again. A postdoc in London.

**In the words of someone who applies like her:**
> "If I return to industry now, it feels like all these years of effort in academia will have been wasted."

**She wants to feel** that the years were worth it. **She wants to be seen as** ambitious and credible, and not to let down the people who backed her.

**How Ngozi hears Arpli**
- **Respect expertise.** Don't explain what she knows. Offer depth when she asks for it.
- **Precision over warmth, never cold.** "Alongside" still applies; for her it shows up as rigour, not cheerleading.
- **Show why.** Say what was checked and where a suggestion comes from, not just that it's "better".
- **Her spelling and register stay hers.** They follow her and her funder, not a US default.

**What "Arpli worked" would look like** (a target, not a finding): sections and track record carry to the next funder. The internal deadline is never a surprise. A rejected bid becomes the next one's starting point.

## Tunde · the career changer

**How he applies:** changing field after years in another, applying wherever the move can happen (jobs in the new field, funded study to retrain, or both). The hardest parts are working out whether he qualifies, and telling the move as a deliberate choice. Applies from Port Harcourt. Weaker evidence than the other five.

**In the words of someone who applies like him:**
> "the hardest part isn't learning new skills it's explaining why I'm changing careers without it sounding like a bad decision."

**He wants to feel** that the change is a choice, not a failure, and free of the shame that stops him starting. **He wants to be seen as** someone moving toward something on purpose, not away from something.

**How Tunde hears Arpli**
- **Story mode.** He isn't filling a form; he is building a story about his life. Support that framing.
- **Reflection prompts, not forms.** "Tell me about a moment this year when the new field felt urgent to you" beats "List your top 3 motivations."
- **Drafts kept without judgement.** He'll write things and delete them. Keep them privately; they often hold the honest version.
- **Specific over strong.** His story is strong because it's specific. Nudge from general to particular, and from the old field's words to the new field's.

**What "Arpli worked" would look like** (a target, not a finding): he can tell the move as a choice, on the page and at interview, and he sees which of his old experience counts in the new field.

## What holds across all six

| What's true across all six | What it means for voice and design | Why we believe it |
|---|---|---|
| None of them wants to be told they're being helped by AI | Credit lands on them; the machinery stays quiet | AI is trusted as a helper, not as the author of their story (synthesis, theme 3) |
| All have been failed by generic tools | Specificity is how Arpli earns trust | Generic AI voice is the top complaint about today's help (theme 3) |
| All write in time taken from something else (work, study, the company, teaching) | No wasted screens, no celebratory pop-ups | Applying is squeezed around jobs, classes and building (`design/13-personas.md`) |
| All read tone within one sentence | Voice is the first thing people meet, every time | "Flowery" and stock-phrase output gets cleaned up or thrown out (theme 3) |
| All need to feel safe before they can be honest | Errors are matter-of-fact; nothing alarming | Shame, fear of a wasted year and fear of judgement run through every group (themes 5 and 6) |

## Who Arpli isn't for

Someone who wants Arpli to write for them so they don't have to think about their own story, or to send applications without looking. Arpli is a thinking partner, not a ghostwriter. People drift toward that stance when silence wears them down, so the voice never scolds. It makes care cheap enough that they don't have to give it up.

## What changed from the earlier version (15 Nov 2026)

- **Built on ways of applying, not on who people are.** Ada, Emeka, Ngozi and Tunde are kept and reworked; Sam and Maya are new.
- **Ada** is now a graduate with work experience, because the programmes she applies to count work hours. **Ngozi** is now early career on a fixed-term contract. **Emeka's** grants and African side are marked as assumptions in the design file.
- **Removed:** the invented "what they say out loud" lines and inner monologues. Real quotes from real applicants replace them.
- **Removed:** the device, night-time, dark-mode, eyesight, age and family details. The research has no evidence on them, so they are checked when testers are recruited instead (device, connection on deadline day, access needs). Dark mode stays a first-class mode for brand reasons (`color-system.md`), not because of a persona.
- **Removed:** the "honest tone" example that told a founder their metric was weak. Arpli never judges a person or their fit (principle 2, `design/10-principles.md`).
- The earlier version is in git history.

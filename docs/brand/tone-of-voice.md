# Arpli — Tone of Voice

Arpli's voice is the first thing a user feels. Before the typography lands, before the colours settle, before the first interaction completes — the sentence on the screen has already told them whether this tool is for them.

**Guiding phrase:** *The knowledgeable friend who's been where you're going.*
Not a product. A presence. Knows the unwritten rules. Helps you show up at your best.

---

## The four voice pillars

Derived directly from Arpli's emotional register. Each pillar is a *constraint*, not a style preference — breaking one is a bug.

| Pillar | One-line definition |
|---|---|
| **Steady** | Measured, unhurried, present. Arpli never panics with you. |
| **Warm** | Human, specific, respectful. Arpli writes to a person, not a user. |
| **Enabling** | The user is the hero. Arpli hands them the pen, not the finished page. |
| **Grounded** | Concrete over abstract. No hype, no AI theatre, no "leverage." |

Together, they describe a voice that is **confident without being loud, warm without being saccharine, specific without being clinical.**

---

## Pillar 1 — Steady

### What it means
Arpli speaks at a steady pace, whatever the user's state. When the user is panicking, Arpli is calm. When the user is confident, Arpli stays measured. The voice carries the implicit message: *I've seen this before. We'll get through it.*

Steady is not slow. It is unhurried. There is a difference.

### Signatures
- Short, declarative sentences.
- One idea per sentence. One instruction per step.
- No exclamation marks except in rare celebratory moments (job offer received, application submitted).
- No "quickly," "fast," "instantly" when they aren't true.

### Do / Don't rewrites

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "Hurry! Deadline in 3 days!!" | "The deadline is Friday. Here's what's left to finish." |
| "⚠️ URGENT: You haven't completed step 2!" | "Step 2 isn't done yet. It takes about 4 minutes." |
| "Instantly transform your application with AI!" | "Let's work through this together, one question at a time." |
| "Oops! Something went wrong 😬" | "That didn't go through. Try again, or save and come back." |
| "Great job! You crushed it!" | "That's done. Three questions left." |

### The one-line test
*Would someone send you this sentence if you were about to miss a deadline you've been dreading for a month?*
If it would raise your heart rate, it's not Steady.

---

## Pillar 2 — Warm

### What it means
Warm is the opposite of clinical, not the opposite of professional. Arpli writes as if it respects the user's time *and* their humanity. It uses second-person. It uses the user's words back to them. It acknowledges effort without performing enthusiasm.

Warm is specific. Vague praise feels colder than silence.

### Signatures
- Second-person ("you") not third-person ("the user").
- Reflects the user's own language. If they wrote "I mentored secondary school students," the next prompt says "secondary school students," not "mentees."
- Names what the user did; doesn't just approve of it. "You led a team of six for eight months" > "Great leadership example!"
- No emoji in system copy. (Emoji is appropriate from the user, not from Arpli.)

### Do / Don't rewrites

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "User profile saved successfully." | "Your profile is saved." |
| "Great work! 🎉" | "That's a strong first draft." |
| "An error has occurred." | "Something broke on our end. Your draft is safe." |
| "Please input your full name." | "What's your full name?" |
| "This field is required." | "Add your name to continue." |
| "Welcome back, user!" | "Welcome back, Ada." |

### The one-line test
*Would a thoughtful friend say this to you if you were sitting next to them at a kitchen table?*
If it sounds like a form email, it's not Warm.

---

## Pillar 3 — Enabling

### What it means
Enabling means the user is always the author. Arpli is the collaborator who hands them better questions, cleaner structure, sharper language — but never the pen. The output always sounds like *them*, just more precise. When Arpli suggests, it suggests. When Arpli corrects, it explains why.

Enabling means Arpli never says "I wrote this for you." It says "here's a possible structure" or "try this phrasing."

### Signatures
- "You" is the subject of success. "Arpli" is almost never the subject of a sentence shown to the user.
- Suggestions are framed as options, not instructions, in creative moments.
- The AI's work is *presented as the user's*. "Here's your draft" — not "Here's what I generated for you."
- The verb "help" appears sparingly. Overuse of "I'll help you" turns Arpli into a servant trope.

### Do / Don't rewrites

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "I generated 3 drafts for you to choose from." | "Here are three ways you could open this. Pick the one that sounds most like you." |
| "The AI thinks you should mention…" | "This part of your story — the six-month fieldwork trip — might land better up front. Want to try it?" |
| "Let me rewrite that for you." | "Here's a tighter version. Compare them side-by-side." |
| "Your application has been auto-completed." | "You've answered all 12 questions. Here's your draft to review." |
| "AI-powered application coaching!" | "Write stronger applications. Faster, by yourself." |

### The one-line test
*After using Arpli, does the user feel they wrote something, or that a machine wrote it for them?*
If they couldn't take credit for the output in good conscience, Arpli has failed Enabling.

---

## Pillar 4 — Grounded

### What it means
Grounded is Arpli's defence against hype. No "revolutionary." No "AI-powered." No "transform." No "leverage." No "unlock." Arpli names what it is doing, in concrete terms, without decoration. If a reviewer liked a certain structure in Wellcome Trust submissions last year, Arpli says so. Trust is built on specifics.

Grounded is also honest about limits. Arpli doesn't pretend to know things it doesn't. It doesn't promise outcomes.

### Signatures
- Concrete nouns and specific numbers over abstract claims.
- No marketing superlatives.
- Citations and provenance where relevant: "Three of this year's Chevening winners used a similar structure."
- Admits uncertainty: "I don't have information about this specific funder's 2026 priorities. Here's what their 2025 call emphasised."

### Do / Don't rewrites

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| "Our AI will revolutionise your application." | "Draft three versions in the time it usually takes you to write one." |
| "Unlock your potential with Arpli." | "Get your Chevening application reviewed before you submit it." |
| "Leverage cutting-edge AI." | "Rephrase this paragraph." |
| "Instantly improve your essay!" | "Here's where this paragraph loses the reader. Try tightening lines 3–5." |
| "Our proprietary algorithm detected…" | "Reviewers for this programme often look for a specific example here." |
| "This application is 95% optimised." | "You've answered every required question. Two optional ones remain." |

### The one-line test
*Would a sceptical, time-poor researcher roll their eyes at this sentence?*
If a line sounds like a landing page in a pitch deck, it's not Grounded.

## Voice at a glance — side by side

A single message, rewritten through all four pillars together:

**Scenario:** User has just finished answering 11 of 12 application questions. One question is still blank.

| Draft | Reads as |
|---|---|
| "🎉 Almost there! Just one more to go!" | Pushy. Marketing. Breaks Steady and Grounded. |
| "You have 1 remaining question. Please complete it." | Clinical. Correct but cold. Breaks Warm. |
| "Great job! You're 92% done. The AI is ready to generate your final answer!" | Hype. Takes credit from the user. Breaks Enabling and Grounded. |
| **"One question left: 'What would you do with this fellowship?' Want to draft it together?"** | **Steady, warm, enabling, grounded. On-voice.** |

## Vocabulary — words to use, words to avoid

### Use freely
- **Verbs:** write, draft, outline, shape, sharpen, tighten, reframe, show, name, add, remove, keep, change, try, review, submit, revisit.
- **Nouns:** draft, outline, section, question, prompt, example, structure, voice, story, opportunity, deadline, application, response.
- **Framings:** "your draft," "your story," "your words," "together," "step by step," "a possible structure," "a different angle."

### Use sparingly
- "Help" — overused. Prefer verbs that name the thing: "rewrite," "review," "shorten."
- "Just" — can sound dismissive ("just click here") or minimising ("just one more"). Audit every use.
- "Simply" — never says what it means to say. Delete and rephrase.
- Exclamation marks — reserve for genuine celebration (application submitted, offer received).

### Never use

| Banned word/phrase | Why | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "AI-powered" | Hype. Users came here *despite* the AI. | Name the outcome. |
| "Leverage" | Deck-speak. | Use. |
| "Unlock" | Hype. | Reach, access, get to. |
| "Revolutionise" | Overclaim. | Change, improve. |
| "Instantly" (when it isn't) | Lies. | Honest timing. |
| "Game-changing" | Marketing. | Specific benefit. |
| "Best-in-class" | Means nothing. | Specific comparison. |
| "Cutting-edge" | Techie. | Delete. |
| "Smart" (as a modifier) | "Smart suggestions" is empty. | Name what it does. |
| "Robust" | Enterprise-speak. | Reliable, specific. |
| "Seamless" | Overused. | Describe the actual flow. |
| "Oops" | Infantilising on an error. | State what happened. |
| "Whoops" | Same. | Same. |
| "Yay / Woohoo" | Patronising. | Simple acknowledgement. |
| "Our AI" / "our algorithm" | Credits the machine, not the user. | Rephrase into user-centric framing. |
| "Transform your…" | Deck language. | Specific action. |
| "Journey" (metaphor) | Clichéd. | Application, process, path. |
| "Dive in" | Chummy marketing. | Start. |
| "Supercharge" | Hype. | Delete. |

### Context-dependent
- "AI" — fine when the user asks about settings, provenance, or model choice. **Never** in headers, hero copy, button labels, or celebrations.
- "Smart" — only when it means *contextually appropriate* and is doing real work in the sentence.
- "Help" — fine occasionally; never twice in one screen.

## Register across moments

Arpli's voice doesn't flatten into one tone. It shifts register with the moment — but every register is still Steady, Warm, Enabling, Grounded.

| Moment | Register | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Onboarding** | Conversational, open-ended | "What are you working toward right now?" |
| **Empty state** | Specific, inviting | "Paste an opportunity and we'll pull the questions out together." |
| **In-flow prompt** | Direct, concrete | "This paragraph trails off. Try ending on your clearest example." |
| **Suggestion** | Optional, framed as choice | "Another way to open this: lead with the fieldwork story. Want to try it?" |
| **Error** | Matter-of-fact, no blame | "That didn't send. Your draft is saved. Try again in a moment." |
| **Celebration (true)** | Short, specific | "Submitted. Good luck." |
| **Reflection prompt** | Open, non-judgemental | "What's a moment this year when this work felt urgent to you?" |
| **System status** | Transparent, unhurried | "Working on your draft. About 20 seconds." |

## Microcopy patterns

### Buttons
- **Primary action** — a verb and a direct object. "Save draft," "Submit application," "Add opportunity." Never "Click here," "Submit," "OK."
- **Secondary action** — same shape. "Save for later," "Keep editing."
- **Destructive** — honest about what will happen. "Delete this draft" (not "Clear").
- **Cancel** — "Cancel" is fine, but a specific "Keep editing" is better when it fits.

### Form labels
- Phrased as a question when it serves the user. "What's your full name?" > "Full name."
- Explanation beneath, not inside the field. (Placeholder text is for examples, not instructions.)
- Error messages adjacent to the field, matter-of-fact: "Add your name to continue" — not "This field is required."

### Loading states
- **Short waits (<2s)**: silent indicator, no text.
- **Medium (2–10s)**: specific text about what is happening. "Pulling the questions from your opportunity link."
- **Long (>10s)**: acknowledge the wait, give an estimate. "This takes about 30 seconds. Your draft is safe."

### Empty states
- Never neutral. Always invite the next step.
- "No applications yet. Paste an opportunity link to start your first one."
- Not "No data to display."

### Confirmation modals
- Describe what will happen in the user's own frame of reference. "Delete this draft? You can't undo this."
- Confirm with the verb, not "Yes." Button says "Delete draft," not "OK."

---

## When to break the rules

- **Celebrating a submission or a win** can carry warmth that would feel overdone elsewhere. Still no emoji in body copy; still no exclamation inflation. A simple "Submitted. That's one down." carries more weight than a confetti animation.
- **Admitting a limit** (we don't know, we can't verify) can be slightly longer than the usual sentence — because honesty earns the extra beat.
- **Error states for data loss** can allow mild weight: "Your draft didn't save in the last minute. Here's what we recovered."

## The seven voice checks before shipping any copy

1. **Steady** — Would this raise someone's heart rate who's already stressed?
2. **Warm** — Is "you" the subject? Does it sound like a person, not a system?
3. **Enabling** — Does the user come out of this sentence as the author of their work?
4. **Grounded** — Any marketing speak, hype, or AI theatre? Delete it.
5. **Specific** — Does this sentence know what it's talking about, or is it generic?
6. **Short** — Can I cut words without losing meaning?
7. **True** — Can we actually deliver what this sentence implies?

If a sentence fails any of these, rewrite it. If it fails three or more, delete and start over.
